## Module 1: Introduction to Financial Statement Analysis (34 Questions)

### Core Concepts

- **Primary purpose of FSA**: Evaluate past performance and current financial position to make economic decisions -- NOT tax compliance, NOT fraud detection.
- **Balance sheet**: Financial position at a **specific point in time** (snapshot). NOT revenues/expenses over a period.
- **Income statement**: Revenues and expenses **over a period** (flow statement).
- **Cash flow statement**: Classifies into **operating, investing, and financing** activities.
- **Notes to financial statements**: Provide accounting policies and supplemental disclosures -- NOT auditor opinions, NOT management forecasts.
- **MD&A**: Management explanation of results, trends, and uncertainties. Treat with **professional skepticism** due to subjectivity.

### Analysis Types

- **Vertical analysis (balance sheet)**: Items as percentage of **total assets**.
- **Vertical analysis (income statement)**: Items as percentage of **revenue**.
- **Horizontal analysis**: Changes in statement items **over time** (year-over-year).
- **Cross-sectional analysis**: Comparing metrics with **peers at the same time**.
- **Common-size income statement base**: Always **revenue** (not net income, not total assets).

### Audit Opinions

- **Unqualified**: Reasonable assurance of no material misstatement (NOT absolute assurance, NOT guarantee of future performance).
- **Qualified**: Material issue in specific area but NOT pervasive.
- **Adverse**: Statements DO NOT present fairly -- misstatements are material AND pervasive.
- **Emphasis of matter**: Draws attention to a properly disclosed matter fundamental to understanding.

### IFRS vs US GAAP Foundations

- **IFRS**: Principles-based (broad principles, more judgment).
- **US GAAP**: Rules-based (detailed guidance, less judgment).
- **Both require**: Accrual accounting, comparative periods, statement of cash flows.
- **IFRS requires**: Only two comparative periods (NOT a five-year summary).

### Key Qualitative Characteristics

- **Fundamental**: Relevance and faithful representation.
- **Enhancing**: Comparability, timeliness, verifiability, understandability.
- **Conservatism and historical cost**: NOT fundamental characteristics.

### Statement Relationships

- **OCI includes**: Unrealized gains on AFS debt securities, foreign currency translation, pension adjustments.
- **Statement of changes in equity**: Reports comprehensive income + dividends declared -- NOT operating cash flows.
- **Proxy statement**: Executive compensation and board composition.
- **Supplementary schedules**: Additional detail like segment data.

## Module 2: Analyzing Income Statements (52 Questions)

### Revenue Recognition (IFRS 15 / ASC 606)

- **Five-step model starts with**: Identifying the contract with a customer (Step 1).
- **Revenue recognized when**: Performance obligation has been satisfied -- NOT when contract signed, NOT when cash collected.
- **Percentage-of-completion**: Revenue based on proportion of costs incurred to estimated total costs.
- **Installment method**: Appropriate when significant uncertainty about collectibility exists.
- **Revenue before cash collection creates**: An asset (accounts receivable).
- **Deferred revenue**: Cash received BEFORE performance obligation satisfied (a liability).

### Earnings Quality Red Flags

- **Aggressive revenue recognition shows**: Higher accounts receivable relative to revenue + increasing DSO.
- **Channel stuffing shows**: High end-of-period sales followed by returns.
- **Increasing net income + declining operating cash flow**: Deteriorating earnings quality (accrual manipulation).
- **High operating cash flow to net income ratio**: HIGH earnings quality (cash-supported).
- **Recording revenue at shipment (not delivery)**: Aggressive recognition -- IFRS 15 requires transfer of control.

### EPS Calculations

- **Basic EPS numerator**: Net income MINUS preferred stock dividends.
- **Diluted EPS**: Always less than or equal to basic EPS.
- **Treasury stock method**: For stock options and warrants (NOT convertibles).
- **If-converted method**: For convertible bonds -- assumes conversion at beginning of period, adds back interest net of tax, adds conversion shares.
- **Anti-dilutive securities**: EXCLUDED from diluted EPS (would increase EPS).
- **Weighted average shares outstanding**: Used for basic EPS.

### Expense and Income Classification

- **Operating income**: Gross profit minus operating expenses (= EBIT).
- **Multi-step income statement**: Separates operating from non-operating items.
- **Discontinued operations**: Net of tax, separately from continuing operations.
- **Unusual/infrequent items**: Within continuing operations, disclosed separately if material.
- **Restructuring charge**: Operating expense, may be non-recurring.
- **Nature-of-expense method**: Classifies by type (materials, labor, depreciation) -- NOT by function.

### Capitalizing vs Expensing

- **Capitalizing costs that should be expensed**: Higher current net income AND higher assets.
- **Share-based compensation**: Reduces net income but NOT operating cash flow (non-cash expense, added back).

### Key Margin Formulas

- **Gross profit margin**: (Revenue - COGS) / Revenue.
- **Operating profit margin**: Operating income / Revenue.
- **Net profit margin**: Net income / Revenue.
- **DuPont ROE**: Net profit margin x Asset turnover x Financial leverage.
- **Interest coverage ratio**: EBIT / Interest expense.
- **Effective tax rate**: Income tax expense / Pre-tax income.
- **Contribution margin**: Revenue minus variable costs.

### Accounting Changes

- **Change in accounting estimate**: Applied PROSPECTIVELY (e.g., useful life change).
- **Change in accounting policy**: Applied RETROSPECTIVELY with restatement.
- **Error correction**: Prior period adjustment (retrospective).

### IFRS vs US GAAP -- Income Statement

- **Research costs**: US GAAP expenses ALL research; IFRS expenses research but CAPITALIZES development costs when feasibility criteria met.
- **Interest paid classification**: US GAAP = operating only; IFRS = either operating OR financing.
- **Goodwill impairment**: NEVER reversed under either framework.
- **Comprehensive income**: Includes OCI items (translation adjustments, AFS gains, pension adjustments) that bypass income statement.

### Inventory Effects on Income

- **COGS under FIFO during rising prices vs LIFO**: LOWER (FIFO uses older, lower costs).
- **Gross profit overstated if**: Ending inventory is overstated (reduces COGS).
- **Overstated beginning inventory**: Increases COGS, UNDERSTATES gross profit.

## Module 3: Analyzing Balance Sheets (21 Questions)

### Classification and Measurement

- **Current assets**: Converted to cash within one year or operating cycle.
- **Working capital**: Current assets minus current liabilities.
- **Classified balance sheet**: Separates current and non-current assets and liabilities.
- **Non-current liabilities**: Bonds payable maturing in 10+ years.
- **Goodwill**: Excess of acquisition cost over fair value of identifiable net assets -- internally generated goodwill NOT recognized.
- **Treasury stock**: Reduction of shareholders' equity (contra-equity).
- **NCI on balance sheet**: Outside shareholders' portion of subsidiary equity.
- **AOCI**: Cumulative OCI items (translation adjustments, unrealized AFS gains).

### Under IFRS

- **PPE subsequent measurement**: Either cost model OR revaluation model (IAS 16).
- **Operating lease liability (IFRS 16)**: Present value of remaining lease payments.
- **Intangible assets with indefinite lives**: Tested annually for impairment, NOT amortized.

### Deferred Taxes on Balance Sheet

- **Deferred tax liabilities**: Taxes owed in future due to temporary differences.
- **Deferred tax assets**: Future tax benefit from deductible temporary differences.
- **Permanent differences**: Do NOT create deferred taxes.

### Liquidity Ratios

- **Current ratio**: Current assets / Current liabilities.
- **Quick ratio**: Excludes inventory AND prepaid expenses.
- **Cash ratio**: Cash / Current liabilities.
- **Current ratio 2.0 + Quick ratio 0.5**: Large proportion of current assets in inventory.

### Key Balance Sheet Ratios

- **Debt-to-equity ratio**: Measures financial leverage and solvency.
- **Financial leverage ratio (equity multiplier)**: Total assets / Total equity.
- **Book value per share**: (Total equity - Preferred) / Common shares outstanding.
- **AR turnover**: Revenue / Average AR (collection efficiency).
- **Days sales outstanding increasing**: Deteriorating collection of receivables.

### Off-Balance-Sheet Items

- **Pre-IFRS 16**: Operating leases and SPEs (special purpose entities) were off-balance-sheet.
- **Post-IFRS 16**: Virtually all leases now on balance sheet for lessees.

## Module 4: Analyzing Statements of Cash Flows I (40 Questions)

### Classification Rules

| Item | US GAAP | IFRS |
|------|---------|------|
| Interest paid | Operating | Operating OR Financing |
| Interest received | Operating | Operating OR Investing |
| Dividends paid | Financing | Operating OR Financing |
| Dividends received | Operating | Operating OR Investing |
| Taxes paid | Operating | Operating (but may allocate) |

### Indirect Method Adjustments

- **Depreciation/amortization**: ADDED back (non-cash expense reduced income).
- **Gain on sale of equipment**: SUBTRACTED (non-operating, reported in investing).
- **Increase in accounts receivable**: SUBTRACTED (less cash collected than revenue).
- **Decrease in inventory**: ADDED (releases cash).
- **Increase in accounts payable**: ADDED (expenses recorded but not yet paid).
- **Increase in deferred revenue**: ADDED (cash received but not earned).
- **Bond premium amortization**: SUBTRACTED (cash paid exceeds expense).

### Direct Method Formulas

- **Cash collected from customers**: Revenue + Decrease in AR (or - Increase in AR).
- **Cash paid to suppliers**: COGS + Inventory increase - AP increase.

### Activity Classification

- **Operating**: Day-to-day business (includes interest paid/received under US GAAP).
- **Investing**: Purchase/sale of long-term assets, acquisitions, subsidiaries.
- **Financing**: Debt issuance/repayment, equity issuance/buyback, dividends paid (US GAAP).
- **Non-cash activities**: Excluded from statement but DISCLOSED IN NOTES (stock dividends, debt-for-equity swaps).

### Free Cash Flow

- **FCF**: OCF minus capital expenditures.
- **FCFF**: Cash available to ALL capital providers after reinvestment.
- **FCFE**: FCFF minus Interest(1-t) plus net borrowing.
- **Capex from balance sheet**: Ending PPE - Beginning PPE + Depreciation.

### Cash Flow Analysis

- **Cash conversion cycle**: DIO + DSO - DPO.
- **Positive net income but negative OCF**: Rapidly growing working capital needs (growing AR or inventory consumes cash).
- **OCF consistently exceeding net income**: Significant non-cash expenses (depreciation).
- **Positive investing cash flow**: Net disposal of long-term assets (more sales than purchases).
- **High capex relative to OCF**: Growth phase requiring investment.
- **Cash flow per share**: US GAAP PROHIBITS; IFRS does not require or prohibit.
- **Cash flow adequacy ratio**: OCF / (Capex + Debt payments + Dividends).

### Method Comparison

- **Direct vs indirect differ ONLY in**: Operating activities presentation.
- **Investing and financing**: SAME under both methods.
- **Combined hybrid method**: NOT acceptable under US GAAP.
- **Cash flow statement must**: Reconcile beginning and ending cash.

## Module 5: Analyzing Statements of Cash Flows II (15 Questions)

### Cash Flow Ratios

- **CFO to total debt**: Ability to cover total debt with operating cash.
- **Cash return on assets**: OCF / Total assets.
- **Cash reinvestment ratio**: Capex / OCF.
- **Fixed charge coverage**: Includes interest PLUS lease payments and other obligations.

### Cash Flow Quality Assessment

- **High quality**: OCF from sustainable core business activities.
- **Low quality signals**: Large positive financing CF (dependency on external capital), consistently positive investing CF (liquidation).
- **Large difference between OCF and operating income**: Large accruals or non-cash items needing investigation.

### Capitalizing vs Expensing Effect on Cash Flows

- **Capitalized costs**: Appear as INVESTING outflows (moves OUT of operating).
- **Expensed costs**: Appear as OPERATING outflows.
- **Same total cash outflow**: Classification changes, not total amount.

### Life Cycle Patterns

- **Mature stable company**: Positive OCF exceeding capex (strong FCF).
- **Growth company**: Negative FCF for several years (heavy investment).
- **Positive OCF but negative total cash flow**: Large investing/financing outflows exceeding OCF.

### Comparability Issues

- **IFRS classification choices**: Reduce cross-framework comparability for interest, dividends.
- **FASB prefers direct method**: Provides more information about cash sources and uses.

## Module 6: Analysis of Inventories (63 Questions)

### Cost Flow Assumptions

| Method | COGS Reflects | Ending Inventory Reflects |
|--------|--------------|--------------------------|
| FIFO | Oldest costs | Most recent costs |
| LIFO | Most recent costs | Oldest costs |
| Weighted Average | Average of all units | Average of all units |
| Specific ID | Actual cost of item sold | Actual cost remaining |

### FIFO vs LIFO During Rising Prices

| Metric | FIFO | LIFO |
|--------|------|------|
| COGS | Lower | Higher |
| Net Income | Higher | Lower |
| Ending Inventory | Higher | Lower |
| Taxes | Higher | Lower |
| Cash Flow (after tax) | Lower | Higher |

### LIFO Mechanics

- **LIFO reserve**: FIFO inventory minus LIFO inventory.
- **Increasing LIFO reserve**: Prices rising, widening FIFO-LIFO gap.
- **LIFO liquidation**: Units sold exceed purchases, using old cost layers -- INCREASES gross profit from older lower costs.
- **LIFO conformity rule (US)**: LIFO for tax MUST also use for financial reporting.
- **Convert LIFO to FIFO inventory**: Add LIFO reserve to LIFO inventory.
- **Convert LIFO COGS to FIFO COGS**: SUBTRACT increase in reserve from LIFO COGS.
- **When prices FALLING, LIFO vs FIFO**: Lower COGS and HIGHER income (reversed effect!).

### Inventory Valuation

- **IFRS**: Lower of cost and NRV (net realizable value). LIFO PROHIBITED.
- **US GAAP**: Lower of cost or market (replacement cost bounded by NRV ceiling and NRV minus normal profit floor).
- **NRV**: Selling price minus completion and disposal costs.
- **IFRS write-down reversal**: PERMITTED (limited to original cost).
- **US GAAP write-down reversal**: PROHIBITED -- new cost basis established.

### Inventory Turnover and Analysis

- **Inventory turnover**: COGS / Average inventory.
- **Days inventory on hand (DIO)**: 365 / Inventory turnover.
- **Declining inventory turnover**: Slowing sales or inventory buildup.
- **Inventory days increasing with flat revenue**: Potential obsolescence or overstocking.

### Inventory Errors (Critical for Exam)

- **Overstated ending inventory**: Understates COGS, OVERSTATES net income.
- **Understated beginning inventory**: Lower COGS, OVERSTATES net income.
- **Overstated ending inventory carries forward**: Following period COGS OVERSTATED (self-correcting over 2 years).

### Cost Classifications

- **Product costs**: Direct materials + Direct labor + Manufacturing overhead (inventoriable).
- **Period costs**: Expensed when incurred (selling, admin, not capitalized).
- **Conversion costs**: Direct labor + Manufacturing overhead.
- **Manufacturing overhead**: Factory rent, utilities, indirect labor (NOT selling commissions, NOT executive salaries).
- **Freight-in**: Included in inventory cost. Freight-out = selling expense.
- **Abnormal waste**: Period cost, expensed immediately.

### Costing Systems

- **Absorption costing**: Fixed overhead INCLUDED in inventory cost.
- **Variable costing**: Fixed overhead is PERIOD cost (expensed when incurred).
- **When production > sales, absorption vs variable**: Absorption shows HIGHER net income (defers fixed costs in inventory).
- **Periodic system**: COGS determined at period end (Beginning + Purchases - Ending).
- **Perpetual system**: Records updated continuously with each transaction.

### Ownership and Special Items

- **FOB destination**: Seller owns goods during transit (until delivery).
- **FOB shipping point**: Buyer owns goods once they leave seller.
- **Consignment inventory**: Reported by consignor until sold.
- **FIFO holding gains during inflation**: Implicitly embedded in gross profit (old costs matched to current revenue).
- **JIT inventory**: Minimize holding costs by receiving when needed.

## Module 7: Analysis of Long-Term Assets (53 Questions)

### Initial Measurement and Capitalization

- **PPE initially measured at**: Cost including ALL expenditures to prepare for use (purchase price + installation + delivery).
- **Interest during construction**: REQUIRED to be capitalized for qualifying assets under BOTH frameworks.
- **Borrowing costs (IAS 23)**: Capitalized for qualifying assets, expensed otherwise.
- **Capitalizing vs expensing**: Affects BOTH income statement AND balance sheet.
- **Higher capitalization**: Higher reported assets AND income in EARLY periods (reverses in later periods).
- **Construction in progress**: NOT depreciated until placed in service.

### Depreciation Methods

- **Straight-line**: Equal expense each period = (Cost - Salvage) / Useful life.
- **Double-declining balance**: 2/Useful life rate applied to BEGINNING book value (ignores salvage until end).
- **Units-of-production**: Based on actual usage/output relative to total expected.
- **Component depreciation (IFRS)**: REQUIRED when components have significantly different useful lives.
- **Depreciation method change**: Change in estimate = PROSPECTIVE application.
- **Land**: NEVER depreciated (indefinite useful life).
- **Leasehold improvements**: Shorter of useful life OR lease term.
- **Depreciation method should reflect**: The pattern in which economic benefits are consumed.

### Effects of Depreciation Estimates

- **Longer useful life estimate**: Lower annual depreciation, HIGHER net income.
- **Higher depreciation expense**: Decreases net income BUT increases OCF (non-cash, added back).
- **Average age of assets**: Accumulated depreciation / Annual depreciation expense.
- **Fully depreciated asset still in use**: Remains on balance sheet at zero net book value until disposed.

### Impairment

| | IFRS | US GAAP |
|--|------|---------|
| Test trigger | Indicators exist | Indicators exist |
| Test method | One-step: Compare carrying to recoverable amount | Two-step: (1) Undiscounted CF vs carrying, (2) Write down to fair value |
| Recoverable amount | HIGHER of fair value less costs and value in use | N/A -- uses fair value |
| Reversal for PPE | YES (limited to original carrying less depreciation) | NO -- PROHIBITED |
| Goodwill reversal | NO | NO |

- **Impairment indicators**: Significant decline in market value, physical damage, adverse changes.
- **IFRS CGU impairment**: Goodwill impaired FIRST, then allocated to other assets pro rata.

### Revaluation Model (IFRS Only)

- **Increases**: Go to OCI (revaluation surplus in equity) -- UNLESS reversing a prior P&L loss.
- **Decreases**: Go to P&L -- UNLESS reversing a prior revaluation surplus.
- **Requirement**: Regular revaluations to keep carrying amount close to fair value.
- **US GAAP**: Does NOT permit revaluation model.

### Intangible Assets

- **Finite-life intangibles** (patents, copyrights): Amortized over useful life.
- **Indefinite-life intangibles**: Tested annually for impairment, NOT amortized.
- **Goodwill**: Tested at least annually -- NEVER amortized, NEVER reversed.
- **Intangibles in business combination**: Recognized separately from goodwill if identifiable (separable or contractual rights).
- **Internally developed software (US GAAP)**: Capitalized AFTER technological feasibility established.
- **Software under IFRS**: Capitalized under IAS 38 when general intangible criteria met.

### R&D Treatment

- **US GAAP**: Research AND development both EXPENSED (except software after feasibility).
- **IFRS**: Research expensed; Development CAPITALIZED when six criteria met (technical feasibility, intent to complete, ability to use/sell, probable future benefits, resources available, costs measurable).

### Other Long-Term Asset Topics

- **Investment property (IFRS)**: Either cost model OR fair value model (IAS 40).
- **Natural resources**: DEPLETED based on units extracted (not depreciated).
- **Depletion formula**: (Cost - Salvage) x (Units extracted / Total estimated units).
- **Asset retirement obligations**: Recognized at fair value when asset is placed in service (PV of future retirement costs).
- **Asset held for sale (IFRS 5)**: NOT depreciated; measured at lower of carrying and fair value less costs to sell.
- **Derecognition**: When disposed of or no future benefits expected.

### Asset Efficiency Ratios

- **Total asset turnover**: Revenue / Total assets.
- **Fixed asset turnover**: Revenue / Average net fixed assets.
- **Company capitalizing more than peers**: Higher assets, potentially LOWER asset turnover ratios.
- **PP&E increasing without revenue growth**: Overinvestment or declining asset efficiency.

## Module 8: Topics in Long-Term Liabilities and Equity (27 Questions)

### Bond Accounting

- **Discount bond**: Coupon rate BELOW market rate at issuance.
- **Premium bond**: Coupon rate ABOVE market rate at issuance.
- **Par bond**: Coupon rate = Market rate.
- **Effective interest method**: Interest expense = Beginning carrying amount x Market rate at issuance.
- **Premium amortization**: Interest expense LESS THAN coupon payment (decreases over time).
- **Discount amortization**: Interest expense GREATER THAN coupon payment (increases over time).
- **Carrying amount at maturity**: Always converges to face (par) value.
- **Zero-coupon bonds**: Issued at deep discount; all return from price appreciation to par.

### Bond Premium/Discount Effects

| | Premium | Discount |
|--|---------|----------|
| Coupon vs Market | Coupon > Market | Coupon < Market |
| Interest expense vs Coupon | Expense < Coupon | Expense > Coupon |
| Carrying value over time | Decreases toward par | Increases toward par |
| Interest expense over time | Decreases | Increases |

### Leases

- **Finance (capital) lease**: Transfers substantially all risks and rewards of ownership. Lessee recognizes asset + liability.
- **Operating lease (IFRS 16)**: Right-of-use asset and lease liability RECOGNIZED (virtually all on balance sheet).
- **US GAAP lease classification**: Finance or operating based on five specific criteria.
- **Short-term lease exemption**: Exists but most leases still recognized.

### Equity

- **Treasury stock (share buybacks)**: Reduces total equity.
- **Retained earnings**: Cumulative net income minus cumulative dividends (NOT equal to cash available).
- **Stock splits**: Increase shares outstanding WITHOUT changing total equity.
- **Convertible bonds**: Both debt and equity characteristics.
- **Callable bonds**: Give issuer right to redeem before maturity.

### Deferred Taxes (Module 8 Context)

- **DTL arises when**: Tax expense exceeds taxes payable currently (book income > taxable income temporarily).
- **DTA represents**: Future tax benefit from deductible temporary differences.
- **Valuation allowance**: Reduces DTA when realization not more likely than not.
- **Statutory vs effective rate difference**: Permanent differences, credits, and rate differentials.

### Leverage and Solvency

- **Debt-to-capital ratio**: Debt / (Debt + Equity).
- **Leverage effect on ROE**: POSITIVE when ROA exceeds after-tax cost of debt.
- **Debt covenants**: Contractual restrictions protecting creditors.
- **Off-balance-sheet liabilities**: Certain guarantees and commitments not meeting recognition criteria.

### Pension Obligations

- **PBO/DBO**: Present value of ALL estimated future pension payments for services to date.
- **Book value vs market value difference**: Market premium/discount for unrecognized intangibles and growth expectations.

## Module 9: Analysis of Income Taxes (18 Questions)

### Temporary vs Permanent Differences

- **Temporary differences**: Create DTA or DTL; REVERSE in future periods.
- **Permanent differences**: NEVER reverse, NO deferred taxes created (tax-exempt income, non-deductible fines).

### Deferred Tax Mechanics

- **DTL most common source**: Accelerated depreciation for tax exceeding book depreciation.
- **DTA common sources**: Warranty provisions, tax loss carryforwards, stock-based comp.
- **Income tax expense**: Current tax payable PLUS change in deferred tax.
- **Tax base of an asset**: Amount deductible for tax purposes in future.
- **Tax loss carryforward**: Creates a DTA (can offset future taxable income).
- **Stock-based compensation tax effect**: Creates DTA (book expense precedes tax deduction).

### Valuation Allowance

- **Applied to**: DTA (not DTL).
- **Effect**: INCREASES total tax expense (reduces net DTA).
- **Trigger**: When realization is NOT more likely than not.

### Tax Rate Analysis

- **Effective tax rate below statutory**: Tax-exempt income, tax credits, lower-tax jurisdictions.
- **Effective tax rate above statutory**: Non-deductible expenses (fines, goodwill impairment).
- **Enacted rate change**: Requires REMEASUREMENT of existing DTL/DTA with impact recognized in current income.

### Balance Sheet Classification

- **IFRS**: ALL deferred taxes classified as NON-CURRENT (IAS 12).
- **Taxes payable**: Current liability -- amount actually due for current period.
- **Increasing DTA over time**: Company paying MORE tax than book expense currently.
- **Non-reversing DTL**: Analysts may treat as EQUITY for analysis purposes.

### Special Topics

- **Uncertain tax positions (US GAAP)**: Recognized if more likely than not to be sustained (ASC 740).
- **Business combination deferred taxes**: Recognized for fair value adjustments to identifiable assets at acquisition date.
- **Intraperiod tax allocation**: Assigns tax to continuing operations, discontinued operations, OCI, and equity.

## Module 12: Introduction to Financial Statement Modeling (28 Questions)

### Model Building Approach

- **Starts with**: Revenue forecasts and growth assumptions (drives everything).
- **Top-down forecast**: Industry/macro growth then market share.
- **Bottom-up forecast**: Individual product volumes and pricing.
- **Revenue growth assumptions**: Supported by industry analysis, company positioning, AND historical trends.
- **Operating expenses**: Projected as percentage of revenue based on historical trends.
- **Model quality depends on**: Reasonableness and supportability of key assumptions.

### Three-Statement Model Linkages

- **Balance sheet links**: To income statement through retained earnings; to cash flow through cash balance.
- **Interest expense**: Average debt balance x Effective interest rate.
- **Circular reference**: Interest depends on debt, debt depends on cash, cash depends on interest -- requires iterative calculation.
- **Depreciation forecast**: Based on PP&E balance and assumed useful life or D&A ratio.
- **PP&E model**: Ending = Beginning + Capex - Disposals - Depreciation.

### Working Capital Modeling

- **Accounts receivable**: Revenue x (DSO / 365).
- **Inventory**: COGS x (DIO / 365).
- **Accounts payable**: Modeled via days payable outstanding.
- **Working capital assumptions**: Days receivable, inventory days, days payable.

### Capital Expenditure and Growth

- **Capex forecasts linked to**: Expected growth and maintenance of productive capacity.
- **Dividend projections**: Payout ratio applied to projected net income.
- **Share count projections**: Account for buybacks, option exercises, new issuances.
- **Effective tax rate in model**: Expected rate considering permanent differences and jurisdictional mix.
- **Deferred tax forecasting**: Understanding expected reversals of temporary differences.

### Valuation and Analysis

- **Terminal value**: Value of all cash flows BEYOND the explicit forecast period.
- **Terminal value approaches**: Gordon growth model [FCF(1+g)/(r-g)] or exit multiple.
- **Sensitivity analysis**: Tests effect of changing ONE key assumption at a time.
- **Scenario analysis**: Changes MULTIPLE assumptions simultaneously (bull/bear cases).
- **Operating leverage**: High fixed costs amplify effect of revenue changes on operating income.
- **Model validation**: Balance sheet balances AND cash flow reconciles.
- **Regression analysis**: Uses statistical relationships between variables for projection.

## IFRS vs US GAAP: High-Yield Differences

> This section is CRITICAL for exam. These distinctions are tested repeatedly.

| Topic | IFRS | US GAAP |
|-------|------|---------|
| **Inventory methods** | LIFO PROHIBITED | LIFO permitted |
| **Inventory write-down reversal** | Permitted (to original cost) | Prohibited (new cost basis) |
| **Inventory valuation** | Lower of cost and NRV | Lower of cost or market |
| **Research costs** | Expensed | Expensed |
| **Development costs** | Capitalized when criteria met | Expensed (except software after feasibility) |
| **PPE subsequent measurement** | Cost model OR revaluation model | Cost model ONLY |
| **Impairment reversal (PPE)** | Permitted (limited to original) | Prohibited |
| **Goodwill impairment reversal** | Prohibited | Prohibited |
| **Interest paid classification** | Operating OR Financing | Operating only |
| **Interest received classification** | Operating OR Investing | Operating only |
| **Dividends paid classification** | Operating OR Financing | Financing only |
| **Dividends received classification** | Operating OR Investing | Operating only |
| **Taxes paid** | Separate disclosure, some flexibility | Operating |
| **Cash flow per share** | Not required or prohibited | Prohibited |
| **Deferred tax classification** | Always non-current | Always non-current |
| **Component depreciation** | Required for significant components | Permitted but not required |
| **Investment property** | Cost or fair value model | Cost model only |
| **Biological assets** | Fair value less costs to sell (IAS 41) | No specific standard |
| **Lease classification (lessee)** | Single model (IFRS 16) | Finance or operating |

## Exam Traps & Common Pitfalls

> Distractor patterns from 351 questions. Review before every mock.

| # | Trap | Rule |
|---|------|------|
| 1 | Confusing vertical with horizontal analysis | Vertical = % of base (assets or revenue); Horizontal = over time |
| 2 | Common-size base for income statement | ALWAYS revenue, never net income or total assets |
| 3 | Audit opinion "absolute assurance" | Only REASONABLE assurance, never absolute |
| 4 | Revenue recognition at contract signing | NO -- requires satisfaction of performance obligation |
| 5 | Cash collection = revenue trigger | NO -- accrual accounting recognizes when earned |
| 6 | Capitalizing inflates current income | YES -- removes expense, creates asset |
| 7 | Diluted EPS > Basic EPS | NEVER -- diluted is always less than or equal |
| 8 | Treasury stock method for convertibles | WRONG -- use if-converted method |
| 9 | Depreciation added back "because it's investing" | WRONG -- added because it's NON-CASH |
| 10 | Increase in AR = cash inflow | WRONG -- AR increase = SUBTRACTED (less cash collected) |
| 11 | Gain on sale ADDED in indirect | WRONG -- SUBTRACTED (reclassified to investing) |
| 12 | LIFO permitted under IFRS | WRONG -- IFRS prohibits LIFO (IAS 2) |
| 13 | Write-down reversal under US GAAP | WRONG -- PROHIBITED (new cost basis) |
| 14 | FIFO = higher COGS during inflation | WRONG -- FIFO = LOWER COGS (uses older, cheaper costs) |
| 15 | Impairment reversal for PPE under US GAAP | WRONG -- PROHIBITED |
| 16 | Revaluation model available under US GAAP | WRONG -- IFRS ONLY |
| 17 | Interest paid is always operating | WRONG -- only under US GAAP; IFRS offers choice |
| 18 | Dividends paid is always financing | Only under US GAAP; IFRS allows operating or financing |
| 19 | Development costs always expensed | US GAAP yes; IFRS capitalizes when criteria met |
| 20 | Goodwill is amortized | WRONG -- tested for impairment, never amortized |
| 21 | Land is depreciated over 40 years | WRONG -- land is NEVER depreciated |
| 22 | Overstated ending inventory = overstated COGS | WRONG -- UNDERSTATES COGS (overstates income) |
| 23 | LIFO liquidation DECREASES profit | WRONG -- INCREASES profit (old lower costs to COGS) |
| 24 | Absorption = variable costing when production > sales | WRONG -- absorption shows HIGHER income |
| 25 | Permanent differences create deferred taxes | WRONG -- NO deferred tax from permanent differences |
| 26 | DTL from warranty provisions | WRONG -- warranty provisions create DTA |
| 27 | Estimate change requires retrospective application | WRONG -- estimates are PROSPECTIVE |
| 28 | Cash flow per share required under US GAAP | WRONG -- PROHIBITED under US GAAP |
| 29 | Negative FCF = imminent bankruptcy | WRONG -- could be growth phase |
| 30 | High capex relative to OCF = strong FCF | WRONG -- high capex REDUCES FCF |
| 31 | Retained earnings = cash available | WRONG -- retained earnings may not equal cash |
| 32 | Premium bond: interest expense > coupon | WRONG -- expense < coupon for premium bonds |
| 33 | Valuation allowance reduces DTL | WRONG -- applied to DTA only |
| 34 | Financial model starts with balance sheet | WRONG -- starts with revenue forecasts |
| 35 | Terminal value = final year cash flow | WRONG -- represents ALL cash flows BEYOND explicit forecast |

## Formula Quick Reference

### Profitability Ratios

```
Gross Profit Margin = (Revenue - COGS) / Revenue
Operating Profit Margin = Operating Income / Revenue
Net Profit Margin = Net Income / Revenue
ROA = Net Income / Average Total Assets
ROE = Net Income / Average Total Equity
DuPont ROE = Net Margin x Asset Turnover x Equity Multiplier
5-Factor DuPont = (NI/EBT) x (EBT/EBIT) x (EBIT/Rev) x (Rev/Assets) x (Assets/Equity)
```

### Liquidity Ratios

```
Current Ratio = Current Assets / Current Liabilities
Quick Ratio = (Cash + Marketable Securities + AR) / Current Liabilities
Cash Ratio = Cash / Current Liabilities
Working Capital = Current Assets - Current Liabilities
```

### Solvency/Leverage Ratios

```
Debt-to-Equity = Total Debt / Total Equity
Debt-to-Capital = Total Debt / (Debt + Equity)
Equity Multiplier = Total Assets / Total Equity
Interest Coverage = EBIT / Interest Expense
Fixed Charge Coverage = (EBIT + Lease payments) / (Interest + Lease payments)
```

### Efficiency/Activity Ratios

```
Inventory Turnover = COGS / Average Inventory
DIO = 365 / Inventory Turnover
AR Turnover = Revenue / Average AR
DSO = 365 / AR Turnover
AP Turnover = Purchases / Average AP
DPO = 365 / AP Turnover
Cash Conversion Cycle = DIO + DSO - DPO
Total Asset Turnover = Revenue / Average Total Assets
Fixed Asset Turnover = Revenue / Average Net Fixed Assets
```

### EPS Formulas

```
Basic EPS = (Net Income - Preferred Dividends) / Weighted Average Shares
Diluted EPS: Treasury Stock Method (options):
  Incremental shares = Options exercised - Shares bought back at avg price
Diluted EPS: If-Converted Method (convertibles):
  Numerator: Add back interest expense x (1 - tax rate)
  Denominator: Add conversion shares
```

### Cash Flow Formulas

```
FCFF = NI + NCC + Int(1-t) - FCInv - WCInv
FCFE = FCFF - Int(1-t) + Net Borrowing
FCF (simple) = OCF - Capex
Capex = Ending PPE - Beginning PPE + Depreciation
Cash from Customers = Revenue + Decrease in AR (- Increase in AR)
Cash to Suppliers = COGS + Inv Increase - AP Increase
```

### Inventory Conversions (LIFO to FIFO)

```
FIFO Inventory = LIFO Inventory + LIFO Reserve
FIFO COGS = LIFO COGS - Increase in LIFO Reserve
FIFO Net Income (approx) = LIFO NI + (Reserve Increase x (1 - Tax Rate))
FIFO Equity = LIFO Equity + (LIFO Reserve x (1 - Tax Rate))
```

### Tax Formulas

```
Income Tax Expense = Taxes Payable + Change in DTL - Change in DTA
Effective Tax Rate = Income Tax Expense / Pre-tax Income
DTA = Deductible Temp Diff x Tax Rate
DTL = Taxable Temp Diff x Tax Rate
```

### Depreciation Formulas

```
Straight-Line = (Cost - Salvage) / Useful Life
DDB Rate = 2 / Useful Life (applied to beginning book value)
Units of Production = (Cost - Salvage) x (Actual Units / Total Units)
Average Age = Accumulated Depreciation / Annual Depreciation Expense
Remaining Life = Net PPE / Annual Depreciation Expense
```

### Bond Formulas

```
Interest Expense = Beginning Carrying Amount x Market Rate
Coupon Payment = Face Value x Coupon Rate
Premium Amortization = Coupon Payment - Interest Expense (when coupon > market)
Discount Amortization = Interest Expense - Coupon Payment (when market > coupon)
```

### Financial Modeling

```
AR = Revenue x (DSO / 365)
Inventory = COGS x (DIO / 365)
Ending PPE = Beginning + Capex - Disposals - Depreciation
Dividends = Net Income x Payout Ratio
Terminal Value (Gordon) = FCF(1+g) / (r - g)
```

## Ratio Relationships: What Moves What

### If Revenue Increases (all else equal):

- Gross profit margin: Unchanged (if COGS proportional) or increases (operating leverage)
- AR turnover: Increases (if AR grows slower than revenue)
- Asset turnover: Increases
- ROA: Increases (higher NI from margins)

### If Inventory Increases (beyond what COGS growth requires):

- Inventory turnover: DECREASES
- DIO: INCREASES
- Cash conversion cycle: INCREASES (more cash tied up)
- Current ratio: INCREASES (more current assets)
- Quick ratio: UNCHANGED (excludes inventory)
- OCF: DECREASES (cash consumed by inventory buildup)

### If a Company Capitalizes More Aggressively:

- Current period: Higher net income, higher assets, higher equity
- Future periods: Higher depreciation/amortization, LOWER income
- OCF: HIGHER (payment classified as investing, not operating)
- Investing CF: LOWER
- Total assets: HIGHER
- Asset turnover: LOWER

### If Depreciation Method Changes from SL to Accelerated:

- Early years: HIGHER depreciation, LOWER net income, LOWER assets
- Later years: LOWER depreciation, HIGHER net income
- OCF: UNCHANGED (non-cash -- same actual cash regardless of method)
- DTL: May INCREASE (if book depreciation now exceeds tax depreciation)

### If LIFO Reserve Increases (inflation):

- LIFO income: LOWER than FIFO income
- LIFO inventory: LOWER than FIFO inventory (balance sheet understated)
- LIFO COGS: HIGHER than FIFO COGS
- Tax savings: HIGHER (lower taxable income)

### If Accounts Payable Increases:

- DPO: INCREASES
- Cash conversion cycle: DECREASES (delays cash outflow)
- OCF: INCREASES (source of cash under indirect method)
- Working capital: DECREASES

## Earnings Quality Red Flags

> Signs of manipulation or aggressive accounting to watch for in FSA.

| # | Red Flag | What It Means |
|---|----------|---------------|
| 1 | Revenue growing faster than AR | Potentially normal, but reverse is worse |
| 2 | AR growing faster than revenue | Aggressive revenue recognition or collectibility issues |
| 3 | Increasing DSO | Deteriorating collection efficiency or stuffing channels |
| 4 | Net income rising while OCF falling | Accrual manipulation -- earnings not cash-supported |
| 5 | Large gap between OCF and operating income | Significant accruals needing investigation |
| 6 | Repeated "non-recurring" charges | May actually be recurring costs being excluded |
| 7 | High-end-period sales + returns | Channel stuffing |
| 8 | Declining inventory turnover + flat revenue | Overstocking or obsolescence |
| 9 | Capitalizing costs aggressively | Inflating current income at expense of future |
| 10 | LIFO liquidation profits | Not from operations -- from dipping into old layers |
| 11 | Income smoothing patterns | Using discretion to reduce earnings variability |
| 12 | Effective tax rate significantly below statutory | Check if sustainable or one-time credits |
| 13 | Revenue at shipment (not delivery) | Accelerating recognition |
| 14 | Large increase in deferred revenue relative to growth | Could signal future revenue recognition |
| 15 | Pro forma adjustments excluding recurring items | Presenting better picture than GAAP allows |

## Decision Frameworks

### How to Approach Income Statement Questions

```
1. WHAT is being recognized? (Revenue, expense, gain/loss)
2. WHEN should it be recognized? (Performance obligation satisfied? Earned?)
3. WHERE does it appear? (Operating vs non-operating, continuing vs discontinued)
4. HOW is it measured? (Historical cost, fair value, NRV)
5. Is it RECURRING or non-recurring?
6. Does it affect BASIC or DILUTED EPS or both?
```

### How to Approach Balance Sheet Questions

```
1. Is it CURRENT or NON-CURRENT?
2. Is it an ASSET, LIABILITY, or EQUITY?
3. How is it MEASURED? (Cost, fair value, amortized cost, NRV)
4. IFRS or US GAAP? (Determines measurement options)
5. Does it create a DEFERRED TAX? (Temporary difference?)
6. Is there an OFF-BALANCE-SHEET component?
```

### How to Approach Cash Flow Questions

```
1. Which SECTION? (Operating, Investing, Financing)
2. US GAAP or IFRS? (Classification differences for interest/dividends)
3. Is it CASH or NON-CASH? (Non-cash = excluded from statement, disclosed in notes)
4. INDIRECT METHOD direction:
   - Non-cash expenses: ADD back
   - Current asset increases: SUBTRACT
   - Current liability increases: ADD
   - Gains: SUBTRACT (reclassify to investing)
   - Losses: ADD back
5. What is the TOTAL CASH effect? (Sum of all three sections = change in cash)
```

### How to Approach Inventory Questions

```
1. Which METHOD? (FIFO, LIFO, Weighted Average)
2. Are prices RISING or FALLING?
3. IFRS or US GAAP? (LIFO prohibited under IFRS)
4. What DIRECTION does it affect? (COGS, NI, Inventory, Taxes)
5. Is there a WRITE-DOWN? (NRV test)
6. Can it be REVERSED? (IFRS yes, US GAAP no)
7. Is it a CONVERSION question? (Use LIFO reserve)
```

### How to Approach Ratio Questions

```
1. WHAT is the formula? (Memorize numerator/denominator)
2. Is the base AVERAGE or ENDING? (Turnover ratios use average)
3. What DRIVES it? (Revenue, COGS, or specific balance)
4. Which DIRECTION indicates better performance?
5. Does the scenario INCREASE or DECREASE the ratio?
6. COMPARE: Is it cross-sectional (peers) or time-series (trends)?
```

### How to Approach Tax Questions

```
1. TEMPORARY or PERMANENT difference?
   - Temporary: Creates DTA or DTL
   - Permanent: NO deferred tax (affects effective rate)
2. Will taxable income be HIGHER or LOWER in future?
   - Higher future = DTL (owe more later)
   - Lower future = DTA (save later)
3. Income tax expense = Current + Deferred portions
4. Rate change: Remeasure ALL existing deferred balances
5. Valuation allowance: Reduces DTA, INCREASES tax expense
```

## Common Calculation Mistakes

| # | Mistake | Correct Approach |
|---|---------|-----------------|
| 1 | Using ending instead of average for turnover ratios | Always use AVERAGE balance for denominator in turnover ratios |
| 2 | Forgetting to subtract preferred dividends for Basic EPS | Numerator = NI minus preferred dividends |
| 3 | Adding AR increase to net income (indirect) | AR increase = SUBTRACT (less cash than revenue) |
| 4 | Confusing DIO formula direction | DIO = 365 / turnover (NOT turnover x 365) |
| 5 | Forgetting depreciation in capex formula | Capex = End PPE - Begin PPE + Depreciation |
| 6 | Using revenue for inventory turnover | Use COGS (not revenue) for inventory turnover |
| 7 | Mixing up LIFO-to-FIFO COGS conversion direction | FIFO COGS = LIFO COGS MINUS reserve increase |
| 8 | Premium bond: saying interest expense > coupon | Premium: Expense < Coupon (expense decreases over time) |
| 9 | Confusing "add back" logic in indirect method | Liability increase = cash SOURCE (add); Asset increase = cash USE (subtract) |
| 10 | Applying tax rate to wrong base for effective rate | Effective rate = Tax expense / PRE-TAX income (not NI) |
| 11 | DDB: subtracting salvage before applying rate | DDB applies rate to BOOK VALUE, ignores salvage until reaching it |
| 12 | Absorption vs variable: wrong direction when production > sales | Absorption HIGHER income (defers fixed costs in unsold inventory) |
| 13 | Cash conversion cycle sign error | CCC = DIO + DSO - DPO (subtract DPO) |
| 14 | Thinking capitalizing reduces OCF | Capitalizing INCREASES OCF (shifts to investing) |
| 15 | Forgetting LIFO reserve tax adjustment for equity conversion | FIFO Equity = LIFO Equity + Reserve x (1 - Tax Rate) |

## Personal Rules (Derived from Distractor Patterns)

1. **The "ALWAYS/NEVER" test**: If an answer says "always" or "never," be suspicious. Exceptions exist for almost everything except: land never depreciated, goodwill never amortized, diluted EPS never exceeds basic.

2. **The "which framework" reflex**: On ANY question about interest/dividends classification, inventory methods, revaluation, impairment reversal, or R&D -- immediately identify IFRS vs US GAAP before answering.

3. **The direction check**: For ratio/effect questions, trace through the full chain. If inventory increases, COGS decreases (if ending), which means NI increases. Go step by step.

4. **The "sounds right" trap**: Distractors often use correct accounting terms in wrong contexts (e.g., "treasury stock method for convertibles," "NRV for initial measurement"). The terminology is correct -- the APPLICATION is wrong.

5. **The dual-effect rule**: Every transaction affects at least two items. If a question asks about one effect, consider whether it also asks "which of these is NOT affected."

6. **The timing distinction**: Prospective vs retrospective is a top distractor. Changes in ESTIMATES = prospective. Changes in POLICY = retrospective. Changes in METHOD (depreciation) = treated as estimate change = prospective.

7. **The operating/investing/financing sort**: Before answering any cash flow classification question, ask: Does it relate to (a) day-to-day business, (b) long-term assets, or (c) capital structure? Then check US GAAP vs IFRS for exceptions.

8. **The "higher/lower income" check**: If capitalizing vs expensing, remember the time frame. Capitalizing is higher income NOW but lower income LATER. The exam can ask about either period.

9. **The carry-forward rule**: Inventory errors self-correct over two years. Overstated ending inventory this year = overstated beginning inventory next year = overstated COGS next year. Net effect over two years = zero.

10. **The non-cash filter**: Non-cash items (depreciation, stock comp, amortization) do NOT affect OCF under either method. They reduce NI but are added back. Same total cash regardless of accounting method.
